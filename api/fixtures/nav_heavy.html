Why Small Databases Win

There is a persistent myth in software engineering that every successful product eventually needs a distributed database. In practice, the overwhelming majority of applications spend their entire lives comfortably inside a single Postgres instance, and the teams that accept this early ship faster and sleep better.

A single well-indexed database keeps the operational surface small. Backups are one command. Migrations run in one place. There is no consensus protocol to debug at three in the morning, and no eventual consistency to explain to a confused product manager.

The boring choice is also the fast one. Modern hardware lets a modest instance serve tens of thousands of queries per second, which is more traffic than most products will ever see. Scaling problems are good problems, and they announce themselves long before they become emergencies.
